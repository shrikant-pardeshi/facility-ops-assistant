# Water Leakage and Seepage Response

## Active leak or pipe burst
1. Isolate the nearest valve on the affected line. If the source is unknown, isolate the floor riser valve.
2. Switch off electrical supply to any equipment or sockets near the water.
3. Protect furniture and IT equipment; place buckets and wet-floor signs.
4. Major water ingress affecting electrical rooms or multiple floors is P1; a single-fixture leak (tap, flush) is P3.

## Seepage and dampness (civil)
Seepage from a terrace slab or external wall after rain usually indicates failed waterproofing, a choked rainwater outlet, or cracks at slab-parapet or slab-column junctions.
1. Check and clear rainwater outlets and gratings on the terrace.
2. Mark the damp patch on the ceiling/slab soffit and photograph it with date for monitoring.
3. Inspect the terrace above for cracks, damaged waterproofing membrane or ponding.
4. Raise a P3 civil work order; repairs involving waterproofing should be planned in dry weather.
5. Persistent seepage near structural members (columns, beams) should be reviewed by a structural engineer to rule out reinforcement corrosion.

## Records and follow-up
Record the location, source, isolation time and damage observed in the work order, with photographs. For seepage, re-inspect the marked patch after the next heavy rain to confirm the repair has worked before closing the ticket.
